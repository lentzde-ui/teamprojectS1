Ireland Travel Guide

Essential practical information and recommendations for your itinerary

Essential Travel Essentials

💶
Currency

Euro (€) in the Republic of Ireland; British Pound Sterling (£) in Northern Ireland.

🚗
Driving

Left-hand traffic. Automatic vehicles are limited and should be reserved well in advance.

🔌
Power Standards

Type G plug (3-prong British standard) operating at 230V / 50Hz. Pack an adapter.

🌧️
Climate

Temperate maritime climate. Frequent light rain—high-quality rain gear is essential.

Seasonal Overview

Season Months Key Characteristics
Spring March – May Moderate temperatures, extended daylight hours, and lower visitor density.
Summer June – August Peak travel season, warmest temperatures (16°C–20°C), and maximum daylight.
Autumn September – November Mild weather conditions, vibrant fall foliage, and reduced accommodation rates.
Winter December – February Cooler conditions (4°C–9°C), shorter daylight hours, and off-season pricing.

Practical Travel Advice

🧥 Layered Clothing

Weather changes rapidly. Pack high-quality, windproof, and waterproof outer layers.

🎟️ Advance Reservations

Top sites like the Cliffs of Moher and Book of Kells require timed tickets booked weeks in advance.

🚘 Vehicle Rentals

Rural secondary roads can be narrow. Opt for full Collision Damage Waiver (CDW) insurance.

💶 Tipping Protocol

Gratuities are appreciated but modest; 10%–15% is customary for sit-down restaurant dining.

Key Regional Highlights

Dublin & The East

The nation's vibrant capital featuring Trinity College, historic Georgian architecture, and world-class national museums.

Galway & West Coast

Famous for lively arts culture, traditional Irish music sessions, local seafood, and boat access to the Aran Islands.

The Ring of Kerry

A renowned 179 km circular scenic route featuring dramatic coastal landscapes, mountain passes, and national parks.

Giant's Causeway & North Coast

A UNESCO World Heritage site in Northern Ireland known for its legendary 40,000 basalt column rock formations.
